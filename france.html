01

Compliance timeline

France's reform runs two parallel tracks — structured B2B e-invoicing and a separate "e-reporting" obligation for everything e-invoicing doesn't cover (B2C, cross-border). Both are phased by company size.

2020
2020In effect
B2G e-invoicing mandatory via Chorus Pro

All suppliers to French public sector entities have been required to submit invoices electronically through Chorus Pro since 2020, ahead of the much larger B2B reform.

January 2026
Jan 2026In effect
Platform accreditation network reaches critical mass

Over 100 Plateformes Agréées (PA) had received DGFiP accreditation by this point, giving businesses a genuine choice of certified provider ahead of the September go-live.

February 2026
19 Feb 2026In effect
Finance Law formally renames PDP → PA and clarifies penalties

Article 123 of the Finance Law for 2026 (Law No. 2026-103) renamed "Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP)" to "Plateforme Agréée (PA)" and amended CGI Articles 289 bis, 289 E, 290, 1737 and 1788 D to set clearer, higher penalties for non-compliance.

September 2026
1 Sep 2026Due soon
Mandatory reception for all; issuance + e-reporting for large/mid-sized businesses

Every VAT-registered business in France must be able to receive structured e-invoices from this date. Large enterprises (>5,000 employees or >€1.5bn turnover) and ETI mid-sized companies (250–5,000 employees) must additionally issue e-invoices and begin e-reporting for B2C/cross-border sales.

September 2027
1 Sep 2027Upcoming
Issuing mandate extends to SMEs and micro-businesses

Small and medium enterprises (PME, under 250 employees) and micro-businesses (TPE, under 10 employees) must begin issuing e-invoices and e-reporting data, completing the phased rollout. This date was reconfirmed in April 2025 after the National Assembly rejected a proposed postponement.

02

File format & data specification

France is unusually flexible on syntax — three different formats are all legally acceptable, as long as they carry the same underlying EN 16931 data.

Accepted formats

HybridFactur-X (PDF/A-3 + embedded CII XML)
Structured XML (Peppol-ready)UBL 2.1
Structured XML (complex B2B)UN/CEFACT CII
Plain PDF / email attachmentNo longer valid from Wave 1

Factur-X is the most distinctly "French" choice — the embedded PDF keeps a human-readable copy for staff who still open invoices by eye, while the XML layer does the machine processing.

Governing standard

European semantic modelEN 16931
National technical specAFNOR XP Z12-012
CoversFormats, codelists, business rules, lifecycle statuses (XP Z12-013)
Ruleset size~112 Factur-X 1.0.06 rules

AFNOR XP Z12-012 is the master reference — it defines the semantic model and syntax for every accepted format plus the e-reporting XML and lifecycle status messages, so any PA or in-house build should validate against it directly.

Mandatory identifiers

SIREN9-digit legal entity ID
SIRET14-digit establishment ID (SIREN + 5)
Reception channelRecipient's chosen PA, per directory entry

Every invoice must resolve to a correct SIRET-level routing address in the national directory — get this wrong and the PA can't deliver the invoice, regardless of whether the XML itself is valid.

Archiving

Retention period10 years
Format to retainOriginal electronic format, guaranteed integrity

Archiving the PDF rendering of a Factur-X invoice isn't sufficient on its own — the original structured file (with embedded XML) is what must be preserved for the full 10 years.

03

Transmission protocol

France's "Y-model" is deliberately decentralised: unlike Italy's single government clearance system, France licenses many private platforms to do the actual invoice exchange, with one shared public directory tying it all together.

Network architecture

ModelDecentralised CTC ("Y-model")
Invoice exchangeVia a certified Plateforme Agréée (PA)
Central directoryPPF (Portail Public de Facturation) — annuaire
Software-only optionSolution Compatible (SC) — must still route via a PA

The PPF itself doesn't exchange invoices anymore — it's the national directory and routing/reporting concentrator. All actual sending and receiving of structured invoices happens through your chosen PA (or an SC layered on top of one).

Lifecycle status exchange

Every invoice must carry status updates through its life — this is what lets DGFiP pre-fill VAT returns in near real time.

DepositedSentReceivedApprovedRejectedPayment DuePayment Done

E-reporting cadence

ScopeB2C sales & cross-border transactions
Legal basisCGI Article 290
Standard VAT regime cadenceWithin 7 days, by 10-day period

E-reporting is not real-time — don't over-engineer for instant transmission. It runs on a 10-day-period cycle with a 7-day submission window, distinct from the invoice-level lifecycle statuses above.

Platform continuity protections

Minimum service continuity1 year after switching PA
PA connectivity requirementMust support Factur-X, UBL, and CII

If you switch providers, your former PA is legally required to keep serving you for at least a year — a safeguard against being locked into a platform that stops fitting your needs.

04

Registering with the central directory

There's no single "sign up here" government portal for invoicing itself — registration happens through your chosen PA, which then notifies the national directory on your behalf.

Choose a Plateforme Agréée (PA)

Select from the DGFiP's published list of accredited platforms (100+ as of January 2026), based on format support, integration with your accounting software, and whether it also handles e-reporting. A "Solution Compatible" (SC) can sit on top of a PA if you prefer your existing software, but it cannot connect to the directory directly.

Provide your company's SIREN/SIRET details

Your PA collects your legal entity identifiers, reception channel preference, and intended e-invoicing go-live date as part of onboarding.

Confirm your directory (annuaire) entry

Review and confirm your company information, chosen reception channel (your PA), e-invoicing address, and service start date. Your PA transmits this to AIFE (Agence pour l'Informatique Financière de l'État) and DGFiP, who populate the national directory used to route invoices to you.

Test format generation and lifecycle status handling

Validate that your ERP or the PA's tooling can generate compliant Factur-X, UBL, or CII output, and that your systems correctly consume and emit lifecycle statuses (Deposited, Sent, Received, Approved/Rejected, Payment Due/Done).

Set up e-reporting for B2C and cross-border sales

Configure separate transmission for transactions outside the domestic B2B scope — these follow the 10-day-period/7-day-window e-reporting cadence rather than invoice-by-invoice lifecycle tracking.

Keep your directory entry current

If you switch PA or change your reception channel, update the directory promptly — your former PA must maintain a minimum one year of continuity, but the directory itself needs to reflect the change so invoices route correctly.

05

Penalties & enforcement posture

The Finance Law for 2026 raised the statutory fines considerably — but DGFiP has publicly signalled a lenient early-enforcement approach.

FailureFineAnnual cap
Invoice not issued/received electronically€50 per invoice€15,000
Missing e-reporting transmission€500 per transmission€15,000
Not connected to an accredited platform (after 3-month notice)€500 → €1,000 → €1,000/quarter—

🕊️ Early enforcement leniency

DGFiP Director General Amélie Verdier confirmed at the May 2026 E-Invoicing Day that sanctions won't be applied automatically from day one — businesses demonstrating genuine implementation effort get a "right to make mistakes" during the initial rollout, with softened enforcement expected to continue at least until January 2027.

⏳ Platform-connection grace period

If DGFiP finds you're not connected to an accredited platform for receiving e-invoices, you get a formal notice and three months to register with a PA before the €50/invoice fine regime kicks in.

Article 123 of Law No. 2026-103 (19 February 2026) amends CGI Articles 289 bis, 289 E, 290, 1737 and 1788 D — this is the citation to check if you need the primary source rather than a summary.
