01

Compliance timeline

There is still no general B2B mandate in Australia — but the federal government has quietly built out a genuinely demanding internal compliance regime for its own agencies.

2018
2018In effect
Australia–New Zealand Government Electronic Invoicing Arrangement

Both governments agree a common approach to e-invoicing — the foundation for the shared PINT A-NZ specification both countries use today.

July 2022
1 Jul 2022In effect
B2G e-invoicing mandatory for Commonwealth agencies

Federal agencies must be able to receive Peppol e-invoices, following Peppol format and communication standards.

2024–2025
15 Nov 2024In effect
PINT A-NZ becomes mandatory

The updated Peppol International specification for Australia and New Zealand supersedes the earlier A-NZ Peppol BIS Billing extension.

15 May 2025In effect
Legacy specifications fully removed

All users must exchange PINT A-NZ — it becomes the only supported specification for sending and receiving invoices and credit notes on the network.

July 2025
Jul 2025In effect
ATO establishes e-invoicing as the default for NCEs

The Australian Taxation Office announces that e-invoicing becomes the default invoice exchange method for Non-Corporate Commonwealth Entities, as part of a broader strategy to cut administrative overhead and speed up payment cycles.

July 2026
1 Jul 2026Due soon
30% of NCE-received invoices must flow via Peppol

Non-Corporate Commonwealth Entities must process at least 30% of received supplier invoices through e-invoicing — a concrete, measurable milestone on the way to full automation.

December 2026
Dec 2026Upcoming
Full automation and quarterly reporting

NCEs must automate the processing and sending of e-invoices, with quarterly progress reports submitted to the Australian Peppol Authority (APA).

Same underlying data model as standard PINT A-NZ billing, but with sender/receiver roles reversed — check with your Access Point whether they support it before relying on it.

What's still voluntary

B2B e-invoicingVoluntary, under the Business e-Invoicing Right (BER)

The BER lets an eligible business request e-invoicing capability from trading partners — a nudge mechanism rather than a legal mandate, and worth knowing about if a customer asks you to switch.

03

Transmission protocol

A textbook four-corner Peppol implementation — no clearance authority, no central government platform for B2B.

The four corners

Every invoice passes through the same sequence:

1. Supplier (ERP)2. Supplier's AP (validates, sends via AS4)3. Buyer's AP (receives, forwards)4. Buyer (posts to ERP)

Message Level Responses (MLRs) provide structured status notifications — accepted, rejected, delivered, or failed — giving full traceability across the exchange.

Governance

National Peppol AuthorityAustralian Taxation Office (ATO)
RoleAccredits Access Point and SMP service providers

The ATO's role here is regulatory oversight of the network, not operating a clearance platform — it never sees your invoice content directly.

04

Getting connected

There's no mandate to register for yet outside government supply — but the onboarding path is identical whether you're preparing voluntarily or supplying a federal agency.

Choose an ATO-accredited Peppol Access Point

Select a certified provider — many mainstream Australian accounting platforms already include this connectivity built in.

Register your Peppol Participant ID

This makes you discoverable on the network so any Peppol-connected trading partner can route invoices to you.

Confirm your ERP generates valid PINT A-NZ XML

Test against the current v1.1.2 specification — anything still targeting the old A-NZ BIS 3.0 profile will no longer be accepted anywhere on the network.

If you supply federal agencies, prepare for the 2026 push

Expect active encouragement (and faster payment incentives) to move onto Peppol as NCEs work toward their 30% target.

Check any state-specific requirements separately

If you supply state government customers, confirm their own adoption program timeline alongside the federal one.

05

Related context

No statutory penalty framework exists for B2B, since there's no B2B mandate — but the trajectory is clear.

🔄 The direction of travel

Between the NCE automation push, expanding state programs, and the Business e-Invoicing Right, Australia is moving steadily toward wider mandated adoption without having formally committed to a universal B2B mandate yet.

💳 Faster payment as the real incentive

Rather than penalties, Australia's approach leans on carrots: compliant e-invoices to government agencies get paid faster under existing payment-terms policy — a commercial reason to switch even without a legal requirement.

🇳🇿 Trans-Tasman alignment

Because Australia and New Zealand share PINT A-NZ, a single integration build serves both markets — genuinely useful if you trade across the Tasman Sea.

📶 Global Peppol compatibility

PINT A-NZ's alignment with newer Peppol markets (Singapore, Malaysia, Japan) means the investment in compliance here also smooths cross-border invoicing into those markets.
