01

Compliance timeline

Finland is a genuine outlier in this tracker: one of Europe's earliest and most mature e-invoicing environments, yet still without any domestic B2B mandate at all — adoption has simply outpaced regulation.

Early 2000s
Early 2000sIn effect
Finland pioneers national e-invoicing formats

Finvoice and TEAPPSXML predate most of Europe's e-invoicing infrastructure — Finland built genuine national capability well before any EU-level push existed.

2019
2019In effect
eInvoicing Act 241/2019 passed

Establishes the statutory right for businesses with turnover above €10,000 to demand e-invoices from their suppliers — a genuine legal right, but not a mandate to issue them.

2020
1 Apr 2020In effect
B2G e-invoicing becomes mandatory

All suppliers invoicing Finnish government agencies must send e-invoices unless otherwise agreed — the mandate that anchors Finland's genuinely high public-sector adoption rate.

2021
1 Apr 2021In effect
State requires EN 16931-compliant e-invoices

Government agencies stop accepting non-standard e-invoice formats — Finvoice 3.0 and TEAPPSXML 3.0 can both produce compliant invoices, but compliance must be actively verified, not assumed.

2025
2025In effect
Peppol Advanced Ordering mandated for Central Government

Central Government now requires Peppol Advanced Ordering specifically, extending standardisation beyond invoicing into the ordering process itself.

2028
2028Upcoming
Government plans to begin ViDA preparations

Finland's government has signalled it plans to begin preparing for intra-EU B2B e-invoicing obligations around this year — a soft, non-binding marker rather than a confirmed mandate date.

2030
1 Jul 2030Upcoming
EU ViDA intra-EU B2B mandate takes effect

Structured e-invoicing and digital reporting become mandatory EU-wide for intra-EU B2B transactions. Finland has indicated it plans to implement at this point rather than moving earlier — a genuinely different posture from most other EU countries in this tracker.

03

Scope: the "soft mandate" explained

The single most important thing to understand about Finland: outside B2G, nothing here is actually mandatory — but the practical reality is that most businesses use e-invoicing anyway.

B2G

StatusMandatory
Applies toAll levels of government
Foreign suppliersMust use EN 16931 format

This is the one genuinely binding requirement in Finland's entire regime — everything else described on this page is voluntary or contractual.

B2B (domestic)

StatusVoluntary — no mandate
Right to demandTurnover above €10,000
Tax authority clearanceNot required

A business can insist on e-invoices from suppliers contractually, and often does — but there's no legal requirement to issue them at all.

B2C

StatusNo mandate

Consumers can receive e-invoices via online banking, Suomi.fi Messages, or Posti's OmaPosti service, but nothing requires a business to offer this.

Intra-EU B2B

Current statusNot yet subject to any Finnish mandate
Becomes mandatory1 July 2030, EU-wide (ViDA)

Finland plans to implement at the EU deadline itself, rather than moving ahead of it the way several other countries in this tracker have chosen to.

04

Getting compliant

There's no registration process to speak of for a mandate that doesn't exist yet — the practical work here is about capability and verification, not sign-up.

If you supply any Finnish public-sector body, confirm e-invoicing capability now

This has been the norm since 2020 — it's not a new requirement, and public buyers will expect it as a baseline.

Verify your Finvoice 3.0 or TEAPPSXML 3.0 output is genuinely EN 16931-compliant

Check TIEKE ry's readiness list for your software, or confirm your operator verifies compliance before transmission.

Use the Verkkolaskuosoite directory to find the right routing for each counterparty

There's no single platform — this directory tells you which operator or portal actually reaches a given organisation.

For domestic B2B, check your specific customers' contractual requirements

Don't assume a legal mandate exists just because e-invoicing is widespread — confirm what's actually required by the counterparties you deal with.

Revisit this page periodically ahead of 2028

Finland's government has signalled ViDA preparations beginning around 2028 — a firmer domestic timeline could emerge well before the 2030 EU deadline itself.

05

Penalties & practical consequences

There's no formal fine schedule to report here, because there's no B2B mandate to enforce — the real consequences of non-adoption are commercial, not statutory.

No B2B penalty framework exists

Since domestic B2B e-invoicing isn't mandatory, there's genuinely no fine or sanction to describe here — a real difference from every clearance or CTC-model country in this tracker.

The real risk is commercial, not legal

A business that can't send e-invoices risks losing out to competitors on contracts with counterparties who demand it — especially larger businesses exercising their statutory right under the 2019 Act.

B2G non-compliance is the one real exception

Failing to send e-invoices to a Finnish government body isn't a matter of choice — this is the one place Finland's regime genuinely functions like a mandate.

Watch the 2028 preparation window closely

Given no domestic B2B date is set, the government's own signalled 2028 preparations are the most concrete early indicator of what might come before the 2030 EU deadline.
