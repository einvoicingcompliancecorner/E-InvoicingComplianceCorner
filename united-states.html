01

Compliance timeline

The US approach has been market-led from the start — a payments-efficiency project born out of the Federal Reserve, not a tax authority closing a VAT gap (the US doesn't have a VAT).

July 2015
Jul 2015In effect
OMB Memorandum M-15-19 directs federal agencies to e-invoicing

Federal agencies are directed to move to electronic invoicing for appropriate procurements by the end of fiscal year 2018, using a federal shared service provider or an approved solution such as the Treasury's Invoice Processing Platform.

2022–2023
Apr 2022In effect
Business Payments Coalition E-invoice Exchange Market Pilot begins

The first wave connects around 20 businesses; by May 2022 over 75 international businesses have joined across three rollout waves, testing secure e-invoice delivery between service providers.

2023In effect
Pilot extends; rules and specifications finalised

The BPC and Federal Reserve formalise the exchange framework's rules and technical specifications, moving from proof-of-concept toward a durable governance structure.

January 2024
Jan 2024In effect
DBNAlliance launches publicly

The Digital Business Networks Alliance — the nonprofit formed by pilot participants — takes over governing the exchange framework, covering both the US and Canada.

Mar 2024In effect
First invoice successfully transferred via the DBNAlliance network

A genuine operational milestone — proof the network functions in production, not just in pilot testing.

02

File format & data specification

There's no federally mandated format — the DBNAlliance network has its own standard, but plenty of US commerce still runs on decades-old EDI instead.

DBNAlliance network format

BaseUBL-based invoices, Peppol-style
StandardsOpen, non-proprietary

The framework deliberately mirrors Peppol's technical approach — a genuine advantage if your business already has Peppol experience from EU or Australia/NZ operations.

What actually dominates today

Large enterprisesWidespread legacy EDI, used for decades
Growing alternativeDBNAlliance network-based exchange

Don't assume "e-invoicing in the US" means DBNAlliance by default — most large-enterprise structured invoicing today still runs on bilateral EDI arrangements, not the newer network.

No mandated content requirements

Federal format mandateNone, for domestic B2B
IRS involvementNone — no e-invoicing requirement of any kind

This is a genuinely different posture from almost every other country in this tracker — there's no tax authority driving format standardisation here at all.

Cross-border reality check

If you trade with the EU, Mexico, Brazil, Saudi Arabia, etc.You'll meet their mandates regardless of US rules

A US company with meaningful international operations will encounter e-invoicing mandates abroad long before any equivalent exists domestically — plan accordingly rather than treating this as a purely domestic question.

03

Transmission protocol

Two genuinely separate channels exist — DBNAlliance for voluntary B2B, and a distinct federal procurement channel for anyone selling to the government.

DBNAlliance's 4-corner model

1. Sender's service provider2. Exchange framework3. Receiver's service provider4. Receiver

A sender and receiver each connect once to their own service provider, and the two providers handle exchange across the network — no bilateral setup needed per trading partner, the same core value proposition Peppol offers in Europe.

Governance

DBNAllianceNonprofit governing the exchange framework
OriginBusiness Payments Coalition E-Invoice Exchange Market Pilot
BackingDeveloped with Federal Reserve Financial Services support

DBNAlliance defines electronic delivery standards, policies, and guidelines, and helps service providers connect to the framework — but it has no regulatory or tax-collection authority whatsoever.

Federal procurement — a separate channel

PlatformTreasury Bureau of the Fiscal Service's Invoice Processing Platform (IPP)
ScopeFederal agency procurement only — not the DBNAlliance network

Don't conflate these — if you sell to a federal agency, you're dealing with IPP or an agency-approved solution, a completely distinct system from the voluntary B2B DBNAlliance network.

Payment-method agnostic

Supported methodsACH, wire, card, instant payments

The exchange framework supports all major electronic payment methods — it's explicitly designed around payment efficiency, reinforcing that this is a payments-industry initiative rather than a tax-compliance one.

04

Getting ready (voluntarily)

Nothing here is mandatory — but given the direction of travel globally, proactive readiness has real upside even without a regulatory deadline forcing it.

If you supply federal agencies, confirm your IPP setup

This is the one genuinely existing obligation here — check you're connected via the Treasury's Invoice Processing Platform or your agency's approved alternative.

Evaluate joining the DBNAlliance network

Look for a member service provider or Access Point if you want to modernise B2B invoice exchange with major trading partners — participation is entirely optional.

Audit your existing EDI relationships

If you already exchange structured invoices via bilateral EDI, weigh whether DBNAlliance's open framework could reduce mapping overhead over time versus maintaining point-to-point connections.

Confirm your ERP's UBL capability

If you decide to join DBNAlliance, check whether your existing software can generate UBL-based invoices natively, or whether you'll need an Access Point provider to bridge the gap.

Build for international mandates regardless of domestic status

If you trade with the EU, Mexico, Brazil, Saudi Arabia, or any other mandating country, ensure your systems can produce their required formats — this need exists independently of anything happening in the US.

Watch your trading partners, not the regulator

Network effects — not compliance deadlines — are what will actually drive US e-invoicing adoption forward. If a major customer or supplier asks you to connect via DBNAlliance, that's the practical trigger to act.

05

Related context

No penalty framework exists because there's no mandate to enforce — the entire posture here is structurally different from every clearance-model country in this tracker.

💰 No federal penalties for non-adoption

This is confirmed repeatedly and unambiguously across sources — there is no regulatory penalty framework for failing to adopt e-invoicing in the US. Any consequences are purely commercial, arising from specific contractual requirements a trading partner or government agency might impose.

🏦 A payments story, not a tax story

DBNAlliance's origin in the Federal Reserve and Business Payments Coalition — rather than the IRS or a state tax authority — is the single most important structural fact about US e-invoicing. The motivation is B2B payment efficiency, not VAT-gap closure (the US has no VAT).

🇨🇦 A North American, not purely national, network

DBNAlliance's framework explicitly covers both the US and Canada — worth knowing if you operate across the border, since the same voluntary network serves both markets.

🔮 The honest outlook

Nothing currently in force suggests a US federal mandate is coming on any fixed timeline. That could change — but the safest planning assumption is that structured invoice data is useful regardless of who requires it, not that a domestic deadline is imminent.
